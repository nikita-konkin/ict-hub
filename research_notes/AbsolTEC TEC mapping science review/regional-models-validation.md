# Regional TEC mapping models, their validation practice, and successors of the Siberian absolute-TEC method (2015-2026)

Research notes compiled 2026-11-17 by a subagent for the AbsolTEC reconstruction project. Scope: verify the three primary cited papers; assess whether the recovered 7-coefficient model (V, Glon, Glat, Qlon, Qlat, Gt, Qt) with slant weighting 1/[S·(1+dt²)] over a ±1 h window and per-station-day fits is standard; compile validation practice and reported accuracies; cover TuRBOTEC/SIMuRG/tec-suite successors and 2021-2026 advances; IRI-2020 accuracy vs GNSS VTEC. Every claim carries a source link; unverified items are flagged.

## 1. Verification of the three cited papers

### Takeaway
All three papers are real and verified with exact bibliographic data. They form one lineage: the 2015 pair (Geomagnetism and Aeronomy; Results in Physics) introduced the single-station absolute-TEC/DCB method ("Siberian method"), and the 2020 Sensors paper (TuRBOTEC) is the method's mature form — and it contains, verbatim, the 7-coefficient model and the 1/[S·(1+dt²)] ±1 h weighting that the AbsolTEC reconstruction recovered.

### Cited Findings
- Paper A (verified via DOI landing page): Yasyukevich, Yu. V., Mylnikova, A. A., Kunitsyn, V. E., & Padokhin, A. M. (2015). "Influence of GPS/GLONASS differential code biases on the determination accuracy of the absolute total electron content in the ionosphere." *Geomagnetism and Aeronomy*, 55(6), 763–769. DOI 10.1134/S001679321506016X — [doi.org landing](https://doi.org/10.1134/S001679321506016X). Crossref lists pages 763–769, but the reference list of the TuRBOTEC paper cites the same DOI with pages 790–796 (ref. [14] therein) — [TuRBOTEC PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/). Page-range discrepancy noted; DOI and authorship are unambiguous.
- Paper B (verified via DOI landing + Semantic Scholar): Yasyukevich, Yu. V., Mylnikova, A. A., & Polyakova, A. S. (2015). "Estimating the total electron content absolute value from the GPS/GLONASS data." *Results in Physics*, 5, 32–33. DOI 10.1016/j.rinp.2014.12.006 — [doi.org landing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rinp.2014.12.006); [Semantic Scholar record with open-access (gold, CC BY-NC-ND) PDF link](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.rinp.2014.12.006?fields=title,authors,year,venue,abstract,externalIds,journal). It is a 2-page communication. Abstract (quoted): "When determining the absolute oblique total electron content (TEC) of the ionosphere using both GLONASS/GPS code and phase measurements, there occurs a systematic error associated with the differential code biases (DCBs). A 1-ns DCB leads to the ~2.9 TECU error when determining L1-L2 dual-frequency oblique TEC. We have developed an algorithm for DCB estimation from the data of a single GPS/GLONASS station. Presented are the results of the algorithm operation compared with the oblique TEC correction by using CODE laboratory DCB data."
- Paper C (verified via PMC full text): Yasyukevich, Y., Mylnikova, A., & Vesnin, A. (2020). "GNSS-Based Non-Negative Absolute Ionosphere Total Electron Content, its Spatial Gradients, Time Derivatives and Differential Code Biases: Bounded-Variable Least-Squares and Taylor Series." *Sensors*, 20(19), 5702. DOI 10.3390/s20195702; received 2020-08-31, accepted 2020-10-04 — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/19/5702). This is the "TuRBOTEC" paper (TayloR-series and Bounded-variable-least-squares based iOnosphere TEC).
- TuRBOTEC model (its Eq. 3, quoted): I_M = S·[ I_V(φ0,l0,t0) + G_φ·Δφ + Gq_φ·(Δφ)² + G_l·Δl + Gq_l·(Δl)² + G_t·Δt + Gq_t·(Δt)² ] + I_DCB — a second-order Taylor expansion in latitude, longitude, and time, with mixed derivatives explicitly neglected ("We neglect the mixed derivatives") — [PMC full text, Section 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- TuRBOTEC weighting (its Eq. 6, quoted): ω_k,ji = Θ(t_k − t_ji + Δt)·Θ(t_ji + Δt − t_k)·(1/S_ji)·[1 + (Δt_ji,k / 1 hour)²]⁻¹, with Δt = 1 h ("Δ t = 1 h is the maximal time difference, for which the data are still used"). The paper states: "The 1/S factor in Equation (6) causes the measurements at high elevations to produce the greatest contribution" — [PMC full text, Section 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- TuRBOTEC per-station-day structure: DCBs are estimated by solving "a consistent set of Equations (7)" simultaneously "for different time instants over 24 h"; "The temporal resolution for t_k may vary from 2 h to 5 min"; elevation cutoff: "we use the data with elevations greater than 10°" — [PMC full text, Sections 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- TuRBOTEC mapping function (its Eq. 4): modified single-layer model, S = [cos(arcsin(R_E/(R_E+h_max)·sin[α(90°−θ)]))]⁻¹ with R_E = 6371 km, h_max = 450 km, and a latitude-dependent correction coefficient α (IRKJ 0.97, NTUS 0.87, THU2 0.94) — [PMC full text, Section 3, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- TuRBOTEC validation results (2014 data, IRKJ station): RMS of VTEC differences vs CODE GIM 1.7 TECU, vs IONOLAB 2.1 TECU, while JPL-vs-CODE GIM differ by 1.5 TECU among themselves; vs Madrigal and SEEMALA-TEC the RMS was 10–12 TECU (attributed to map-cell vs single-station methods); IRI-2012 simulation recovery RMS: IRKJ 0.09 TECU, NTUS 0.35 TECU, THU2 0.17 TECU; simulated DCB recovery max error 0.6 TECU, RMS 0.3 TECU; real GPS DCBs vs CODE max 5 TECU / RMS 2.4 TECU, real GLONASS DCBs vs CODE max 17 TECU / RMS 8.8 TECU; ~9% of constrained VTECs differ by >1 TECU from the unconstrained solution — [PMC full text, Sections 4.1–4.4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).

### Inferences
- The reconstruction's recovered formulation (7 coefficients; 1/[S(1+dt²)]; ±1 h window; per-station-day DCB fits) is not an arbitrary choice: it reproduces TuRBOTEC Eqs. 3 and 6 nearly verbatim. The original 2017 "AbsolTEC" executable was very likely an implementation of the Irkutsk method family (2015 G&A / RINP papers → TuRBOTEC 2020), possibly the pre-publication version referenced in TuRBOTEC as "the previous version of the suggested technique (without constrains and several other issues) … experimentally checked in [De La Luz et al. 2018; Sergeeva et al. 2017]" — [PMC full text, Section 4.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- If the reconstruction lacks the bounded-variable (non-negativity) constraint, it matches the *older* variant, not TuRBOTEC's final form: the unconstrained variant produced negative slant TEC on some satellites (TuRBOTEC Fig. 15) — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).

### Gaps
- Full text of the 2015 Geomagnetism and Aeronomy paper could not be opened (Springer paywall/bot-block; Semantic Scholar lists the abstract as elided) — its internal model details were inferred from TuRBOTEC and the RINP abstract rather than quoted directly.
- Page-range discrepancy for Paper A (763–769 per Crossref vs 790–796 per TuRBOTEC ref. 14) unresolved; both variants circulate in the literature.

## 2. Is a local quadratic polynomial (7 coefficients) a standard regional TEC model?

### Takeaway
Yes as a method class, but the exact 7-coefficient per-station space-time quadratic is the TuRBOTEC formulation, which is itself rooted in a long, citable lineage (Lanyi & Roth 1988; Sardón & Zarraoa 1997; Schaer 1999). The broader literature uses polynomial/Taylor, trigonometric, spherical harmonic, spherical cap harmonic, B-spline, multi-surface, and kriging formulations; several assessment papers put polynomial models among the standard choices, usually expanded in sun-fixed coordinates.

### Cited Findings
- Lineage of polynomial/Taylor TEC mapping (as traced by TuRBOTEC's introduction): "For the first time, this method was offered by Lanyi and Roth [1988] and by Sardón and Zarraoa [1997]"; "the Taylor series expansion … on space was mentioned by Schaer [1999 PhD thesis] for regional vertical TEC modeling"; Ma & Maruyama (2003) used the zero-order approximation I_V = I_V(φ0, l0, t0) over a station (no gradients) — [TuRBOTEC PMC full text, Section 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/); original refs: Lanyi & Roth 1988, *Radio Sci.* 23:483–492, DOI 10.1029/RS023i004p00483; Sardón & Zarraoa 1997, *Radio Sci.* 32:1899–1910, DOI 10.1029/97RS01457; Schaer 1999, Ph.D. thesis, Univ. Bern; Ma & Maruyama 2003, *Ann. Geophys.* 21:2083–2093, DOI 10.5194/angeo-21-2083-2003 — [TuRBOTEC reference list](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- TuRBOTEC justifies the second order explicitly: IRI-2012 simulation shows "using the second order of the I_V expansion into the Taylor series, viz., using of time derivatives and square gradients on space, considerably improves the absolute vertical TEC recovery accuracy", and second order "provides a sufficient accuracy to be able to neglect higher orders" — [PMC full text, Section 3, Fig. 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- A dedicated model intercomparison exists: Li, B., Wang, M., Wang, Y., Guo, H. (2019). "Model assessment of GNSS-based regional TEC modeling: polynomial, trigonometric series, spherical harmonic and multi-surface function." *Acta Geodaetica et Geophysica*, 54(3), 333–357. DOI 10.1007/s40328-019-00262-8 — [GFZ public record](https://gfzpublic.gfz.de/pubman/item/item_4709898); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40328-019-00262-8). From searchable snippets: their expansions use geographic latitude and "sun angle at IPP" relative to the regional center (sun-fixed coordinates), and they compare RMS accuracies of the four model types against bias-corrected L4-TEC, with the multi-surface function showing apparent differences (best performance) — [OUCI full-text mirror snippet](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/40zOEQo9). (Abstract is elided by the publisher; details above are from snippet-level evidence only.)
- Xiong, B. et al. (2023). "Constructing a Regional Ionospheric TEC Model in China with Empirical Orthogonal Function." *Remote Sensing*, 15(21), 5207 — states "the methods of constructing regional ionospheric TEC model using GNSS data mainly include trigonometric series, polynomial …" — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/21/5207).
- Yang, H. & Gao, Y. (2015). "Regional TEC Modelling Based on Combined GPS/BeiDou." ION GNSS+ 2015 — polynomial regional model with GPS+BeiDou; "one IFB datum … is added as virtual observation to solve the problem of Matrix rank deficiency" — [ION abstract page](https://ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=12833) (522 error during fetch; citation corroborated by [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395685037_Ionospheric_TEC_forecasting_based_on_Transformer-LSTM_ensemble_algorithm) listing "Conference Paper, Nov 2015, Hongzhou Yang, Yang Gao").
- Li, W. et al. (2022). "A satellite-based method for modeling ionospheric slant TEC from GNSS observations: algorithm and validation." *GPS Solutions*, 26. DOI 10.1007/s10291-021-01191-2 — "The polynomial coefficients can be estimated with the Weighted Least Squares (WLS) method and an elevation-dependent weight matrix" — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-021-01191-2); [open PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10291-021-01191-2.pdf).
- Conference example: Li, W. et al. (2020). "Establishment of polynomial regional ionospheric delay model by using GNSS." *J. Phys. Conf. Ser.*, 1550, 042057 — [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1550/4/042057/pdf).
- Alternatives with citable pedigree: spherical cap harmonics — Liu, J., Chen, R., Wang, Z., Zhang, H. (2011). "Spherical cap harmonic model for mapping and predicting regional TEC." *GPS Solutions*, 15(2), 109–119. DOI 10.1007/s10291-010-0174-8 (cited ~160 times; "the spherical cap harmonic model has a better TEC mapping accuracy with smoother residual distributions in both temporal and spatial …" vs alternatives) — [ACM/Springer](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/s10291-010-0174-8); B-splines — Durmaz, M. & Karslioglu, M. O. (2014). "Regional vertical total electron content (VTEC) modeling together with satellite and receiver differential code biases (DCBs) using semi-parametric multivariate adaptive regression B-splines (SP-BMARS)." *J. Geod.*, 89, 347–360. DOI 10.1007/s00190-014-0779-8 — [TuRBOTEC ref. 31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/); kriging — "The kriging method of TEC instantaneous mapping", *Adv. Space Res.*, 2002, DOI 10.1016/S0273-1177(02)00050-9 (authors not captured in this session) — [ScienceDirect DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0273-1177%2802%2900050-9).
- The reconstruction's time dimension is also standard practice: TuRBOTEC fits G_t and Gq_t explicitly (Eq. 3) and its Fig. 12 validates the recovered dTEC/dt against CODE/JPL GIM-derived derivatives for quiet (Kp_max=2.3) and storm (Kp_max=7.7) days — [PMC full text, Section 4.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).

### Inferences
- The specific 7-coefficient per-station quadratic is defensible as peer-reviewed (TuRBOTEC), simulation-justified (second order "considerably improves" recovery; higher orders negligible), and lineage-consistent (Schaer's regional Taylor suggestion; Lanyi & Roth polynomials).
- However, "standard regional practice" in the wider literature more often means: polynomial in sun-fixed coordinates (Li et al. 2019), trigonometric/spherical-harmonic/cap-harmonic functions for larger regions, or B-splines/MSF — per-station Taylor fits are a niche for sparse networks, exactly the use case TuRBOTEC argues for.

### Gaps
- The Li et al. 2019 full text (abstract elided, paywalled) could not be read to quote which polynomial orders they tested and why; only snippet-level statements are reported.
- No paper was found that directly benchmarks the 7-coefficient quadratic against, e.g., SHC or B-splines on identical data; the choice is justified within TuRBOTEC by IRI simulation only.

## 3. Regional models vs global maps: station density, sparse geometry, conditioning/rank

### Takeaway
The literature is consistent: GIMs lose reliability where stations are sparse, and per-station or regional fits are then preferable; but local polynomial fits have a limited spatial validity radius (TuRBOTEC quantifies >10 TECU errors beyond 10–15° in latitude), and combined TEC+DCB estimation is rank-deficient without datum constraints (zero-mean DCB, virtual IFB observations). Regularization in the literature appears as inequality constraints (BVLS), background-model priors (Kalman filter with IRI), and datum conditions rather than explicit ridge/Tikhonov terms.

### Cited Findings
- Sparse-region motivation (TuRBOTEC): "When using the data from sparse networks of stations, a more promising way is to build TEC maps … For regions with a small number of stations, it makes sense to estimate the vertical TEC over the station"; Madrigal cell-based maps are unreliable "in the regions, where there are few stations" — [PMC full text, Sections 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Spatial validity radius of a per-station fit (TuRBOTEC Fig. 10, IRI-2012 simulation): moving away from IRKJ, TEC estimate errors "at distances more than 10°–15°, … start to surpass 10 TECU"; latitude errors grow faster than longitude errors; gradient estimates are "substantially lower" accuracy than VTEC, with latitude-gradient errors up to 1 TECU/deg at equatorial stations — [PMC full text, Sections 4.2 and Conclusions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Sparse-geometry symptom: at high-latitude THU2 "there is … absence of satellite observations at high elevations in high latitude regions, and … dominance of the southward contribution to the total measurement statistics", which degrades the fit — [PMC full text, Section 4.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Rank deficiency: combined regional TEC + satellite/receiver-bias systems need a datum — Yang & Gao (2015) add "one IFB datum … as virtual observation to solve the problem of Matrix rank deficiency" — [ION abstract](https://ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=12833); TuRBOTEC applies the zero-mean satellite-DCB condition per constellation (Eq. 9) following Schaer's SINEX-BIAS convention — [PMC full text, Section 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Inequality-constrained least squares as regularization: Start & Parker (1995) bounded-variable LS algorithm (implemented as scipy.optimize.lsq_linear) enforces I_V > 0.5 TECU and DCB bounds — [PMC full text, Section 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/); earlier use of inequality-constrained LS to eliminate negative VTEC in GIMs: Zhang, H., Xu, P., Han, W., Ge, M., Shi, C. (2013). *Adv. Space Res.* 51:988–1000. DOI 10.1016/j.asr.2012.06.026 — [TuRBOTEC ref. 38](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Background-model regularization in an operational regional mapper: the Australian Bureau of Meteorology (Space Weather Services) TEC mapping uses spherical harmonics "defined over a region rather than the entire globe", with "a Kalman Filter to find the optimal set of co-efficients given the GPS TEC and IRI model data" — i.e., IRI acts as a prior/regularizer — [BoM SWS page](https://sws.bom.gov.au/Satellite/6/2).
- Station-density dependence of accuracy: Moses et al. (2020), deep neural network TEC over Africa: "the model performance diminishes by up to 18% of the performance within the training area" outside the training region; recommends more ground stations — [ScienceDirect](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1674984720300537).
- GIM reliability vs latitude: Tornatore, V. et al. (2021). "Performance Evaluation of VTEC GIMs for Regional Applications…": "TEC derived by IGS GIMs seems to be more reliable at mid latitudes than at low latitudes" — [GFZ PDF](https://gfzpublic.gfz.de/pubman/item/item_5006792_2/component/file_5006845/5006792.pdf?mode=download).
- Where stations are dense, GIMs are good: "the average RMS of IGS GIM over China is less than 2 TECu" (vs dense CMONOC network VTEC) — "Assessment and analysis of the global ionosphere maps over China based on CMONOC GNSS data", *Frontiers in Earth Science*, 2023 — [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1095754/full).

### Inferences
- For the AbsolTEC use case (single station or a small Siberian network), the per-station model choice is consistent with TuRBOTEC's stated rationale; but the reconstruction's outputs should not be extrapolated beyond ~10° (~1000 km) from a station, per TuRBOTEC's own error-growth results.
- The daily per-station-day DCB solve implies a rank-deficiency handling (either zero-mean per constellation or a virtual datum); if the reconstructed program does not apply one, its DCB output is ambiguous up to a constant per constellation — worth checking against the original executable's behavior.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying the condition number of the 7-coefficient per-station normal matrix was found; conditioning concerns in the literature are handled via datum constraints and BVLS, not via published condition-number analyses.
- SIMuRG's own VTEC-map spatial interpolation method could not be confirmed from full text (Springer bot-block); abstract-level facts only (see Section 6).

## 4. Weighting schemes in regional TEC fitting

### Takeaway
The recovered 1/[S(1+dt²)] slant-space weighting with a ±1 h hard window is exactly TuRBOTEC's Eq. 6 and is simulation/paper-justified (1/S upweights high elevations; the Lorentzian-in-time term smooths the fit; 1 h matches the data window). The wider literature confirms elevation-dependent weighting as standard practice, but a systematic peer-reviewed comparison of 1/S vs 1/S² vs sin(e) weighting was not found.

### Cited Findings
- TuRBOTEC weighting, verbatim (Eq. 6): ω = Θ(t_k−t_ji+Δt)·Θ(t_ji+Δt−t_k)·(1/S_ji)·[1+(Δt_ji,k/1 h)²]⁻¹, Δt = 1 h; rationale stated: "The 1/S factor in Equation (6) causes the measurements at high elevations to produce the greatest contribution" — [PMC full text, Section 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Elevation-dependent weights in a polynomial slant-TEC model (independent of the Irkutsk group): Li, W. et al. (2022), *GPS Solutions* — "The polynomial coefficients can be estimated with the Weighted Least Squares (WLS) method and an elevation-dependent weight matrix" — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-021-01191-2).
- Weighted least squares for bias estimation is described as standard: Vierinen, J. et al. (2016). "Statistical framework for estimating GNSS bias": "the TEC differences are weighted in the linear least-squares minimization procedure, which is used to produce the bias estimates" — [open copy](https://d-nb.info/1142275086/34).
- Mapping-function choice affects effective slant weighting and is itself an active research topic: TuRBOTEC uses the modified single-layer model with a latitude-dependent α (Eq. 4) and shows α matters below ~30° elevation — [PMC full text, Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/); alternative: the Barcelona Ionospheric Mapping Function (Lyu et al. 2018, *GPS Solutions* 22:67, DOI 10.1007/s10291-018-0731-0); NeQuick-based obliquity factors "did not provide an essential accuracy increase" (Schüler & Oladipo 2013, *GPS Solutions*, DOI 10.1007/s10291-013-0315-y) — [TuRBOTEC refs. 53–54](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Elevation cutoff interacts with weighting: TuRBOTEC uses "data with elevations greater than 10°" — [PMC full text, Section 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).

### Inferences
- The reconstruction's weighting is standard-within-this-method: it matches TuRBOTEC exactly, and the literature independently confirms elevation-dependent weighting as common practice. The time-window taper [1+(Δt/1h)²]⁻¹ is specific to TuRBOTEC (not found verbatim in other papers) but has a clear paper-stated purpose (temporal localization, Fig. 5 of the paper shows the window's effect).
- The 1/S (not 1/S²) choice means the weighting acts in slant space so that residual minimization is approximately in VTEC space — consistent with the model being written in terms of slant TEC (Eq. 3).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed intercomparison of 1/S vs 1/S² vs sin(e) vs σ⁻² elevation weighting for regional TEC mapping was found in this session; the choice cannot be independently cross-checked against a dedicated study, only against practice (elevation-dependent weights) and TuRBOTEC's internal rationale.

## 5. Validation practice and reported accuracies (RMSE in TECU)

### Takeaway
Standard validation stack: (1) simulation against IRI/IRI-plas (sub-TECU internal consistency), (2) comparison against IGS/CODE GIM VTEC (1–3 TECU for good regional models), (3) dSTEC arc consistency (sub-0.1 TECU precision check, gives model accuracies of 3–5 TECU), (4) Jason altimetry VTEC over oceans (~4–6 TECU differences), and occasionally ionosondes/ISR (rarely used for TEC-map validation; mostly for profiles). Reported regional-model accuracies cluster at 1.5–5.5 TECU; GIMs themselves are ~2 TECU where stations are dense.

### Cited Findings
- TuRBOTEC's validation design (the direct template for the reconstruction): IRI-2012 + IRI-plas simulation; comparisons with CODE and JPL GIMs (2 h), IONOLAB (Arikan et al. 2007 regularized single-station TEC, *Adv. Space Res.* 39:867–874, DOI 10.1016/j.asr.2007.01.082), SEEMALA-TEC, Madrigal. Results: CODE-vs-TuRBOTEC RMS 1.7 TECU; IONOLAB-vs-TuRBOTEC 2.1 TECU; JPL-vs-CODE (inter-GIM) 1.5 TECU; Madrigal and SEEMALA-TEC differ by 10–12 TECU from TuRBOTEC; simulation RMS 0.09–0.35 TECU — [PMC full text, Sections 2, 4.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- GIM benchmark: IGS GIM RMS < 2 TECU over dense-network China — [Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1095754/full); WHU-GIM dSTEC accuracy 3.14 TECU over China 2008–2020 — [He et al. 2023, *Space Weather*, DOI 10.1029/2023SW003646](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023SW003646).
- Regional models vs GIM: an Egyptian/regional study reports "the estimated VTEC values obtained from the developed models agree to within 4 TECU (RMS) with the IGS-GIM" — Abdelazeem & Celik et al. 2018, FIG Congress proceedings (conference, weaker source) — [PDF](http://fig.net/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig2018/papers/ts02e/TS02E_abdelazeem_celik_et_al_9238.pdf).
- Machine-learning regional models: Africa deep network RMSE 5.5 TECU (SD 1.5) vs GIM — [Moses et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1674984720300537); global NN TEC forecast RMSE 3.5 TECU vs IGS GIM — [Cesaroni et al. 2020, *J. Space Weather Space Clim.*](https://www.swsc-journal.org/articles/swsc/full_html/2020/01/swsc190061/swsc190061.html); China TEC-map forecasting RMSE 1.71 TECU / MAE 1.22 TECU — [Tang et al. 2023, *Remote Sensing* 15(13):3405](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/13/3405); NeuralProphet-based regional prediction RMSE 2.33 TECU — [*Remote Sensing* 15(1):195, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15010195).
- Altimetry validation: multi-instrument study reports Jason VTEC RMSE ≈ 5.5 TECU (quiet) — "Analysis of Ionospheric VTEC Retrieved from Multi-Instrument Observations", *Atmosphere* 15(6):697, 2024 — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos15060697); a 2025 hybrid data-assimilation study validates against "GIM grids and JASON-3 altimetry VTEC … standard deviation of 4.7 TECU and 5.57 TECU, respectively" — [JGR Space Physics, DOI 10.1029/2025ja034893](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ja034893); intercomparison of RO/GIM/altimetry VTEC with Jason-1 as benchmark — "Differences among the total electron content derived by radio occultation, global ionospheric maps and satellite altimetry", *J. Geod.*, 2024, DOI 10.1007/s00190-024-01893-8 — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00190-024-01893-8); GIM validation vs altimetry for 2014/2018 — "Validation of GNSS-derived global ionosphere maps for different solar activity levels", *GPS Solutions*, 2021, DOI 10.1007/s10291-021-01142-x — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-021-01142-x); newest: real-time vs postprocessed IGS GIMs validated with Jason-3 altimetry and DORIS dSTEC — *GPS Solutions*, 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10291-026-02022-y — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-026-02022-y).
- dSTEC as a model-accuracy metric: He et al. 2023 use phase-arc differential slant TEC "with about 0.1 TECU accuracy" to score WHU-GIM (3.14 TECU) and IRI-2020 (4.57 TECU) — [abstract via Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1029/2023SW003646?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,venue).
- Ionosonde/ISR: in the papers retrieved, ionosondes and ISR appear as absolute-TEC/profile ground truth for tomography and profile work (e.g., Mitchell & Spencer 2003; Jin & Park 2007; Potekhin et al. 2009 — [TuRBOTEC refs. 19–24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)), not as the routine validation instrument for regional TEC-map RMSE in the 2015–2026 papers found.

### Inferences
- The reconstruction's natural external checks, in order of tightness: (a) dSTEC arc-consistency (cheap, rigorous, no external data needed), (b) CODE GIM VTEC at the station (expect ~1.5–3 TECU agreement; inter-GIM spread itself is ~1.5 TECU), (c) IRI/IRI-plas simulation echo-tests (sub-TECU), (d) IRI-2020 as a coarse plausibility check only (Section 8).
- The TuRBOTEC-vs-Madrigal/SEEMALA 10–12 TECU discrepancy is a caution: comparing the reconstruction against a single-station product of a different method class can produce large apparent errors that are method differences, not errors — validation should use several independent references.

### Gaps
- No 2021–2026 paper reporting RMSE of a per-station Taylor/TuRBOTEC-type mapper against altimetry specifically (altimetry validations found are for GIMs/assimilation products).
- Ionosonde/ISR VTEC comparisons for regional models: not found in this session (common in tomography literature instead).

## 6. The TuRBOTEC successor (2020 Sensors) and open-source availability

### Takeaway
TuRBOTEC is a direct evolution of the 2015 Siberian method: same single-station absolute-TEC goal, but adds the full second-order space-time Taylor model (gradients + time derivatives), bounded-variable least squares for non-negativity, per-constellation zero-mean DCBs, and multi-GNSS support. Its slant-TEC processing toolchain (tec-suite) and the Irkutsk group's data system (SIMuRG) are open; the TuRBOTEC mapper itself was not found as public source code.

### Cited Findings
- What changed vs 2015: the 2015 RINP paper estimates station DCBs + absolute slant TEC from a single GPS/GLONASS station ("algorithm for DCB estimation from the data of a single GPS/GLONASS station", comparison with CODE) — [abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.rinp.2014.12.006?fields=title,authors,year,venue,abstract,externalIds,journal); TuRBOTEC 2020 adds the second-order Taylor expansion with spatial gradients and time derivatives, BVLS non-negativity of VTEC and slant TEC (I_V > 0.5 TECU), α-corrected MSLM mapping, GPS/GLONASS/Galileo/BeiDou + GEO capability, and the phase-only fallback (Eq. 10) when intra-day DCB jumps destroy the pseudorange solution — [PMC full text, Sections 3, 4.5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- Published successor application (updating ionospheric models with this class of TEC): Kotova, D. S., Ovodenko, V. B., Yasyukevich, Y. V., et al. (2019). "Efficiency of updating the ionospheric models using total electron content at mid- and sub-auroral latitudes." *GPS Solutions*, 24:25. DOI 10.1007/s10291-019-0936-x — [TuRBOTEC ref. 61](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/).
- tec-suite (open source, GPL-3.0, v0.7.8, copyright 2017 Ilya Zhivetiev, gnss-lab): "Software to reconstruct slant total electron content (TEC) value in the ionosphere using data derived from global navigation satellite systems such as GPS, GLONASS, etc."; supports GPS/GLONASS/Galileo/BeiDou/GEO/IRNSS, RINEX 2/3, Hatanaka; Windows/Linux/macOS binaries; docs at tec-suite.readthedocs.io — [GitHub](https://github.com/gnss-lab/tec-suite). Note: tec-suite computes/levels slant TEC; it is not itself the 7-coefficient mapping fitter.
- SIMuRG (open service + open data): Yasyukevich, Y. V., Kiselev, A. V., Zhivetiev, I. V., Edemskiy, I. K., Syrovatskii, S. V., Maletckii, B. M., Vesnin, A. M. (2020). "SIMuRG: System for Ionosphere Monitoring and Research from GNSS." *GPS Solutions*, 24(3):69. DOI 10.1007/s10291-020-00983-2 — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-020-00983-2). Service at simurg.iszf.irk.ru / simurg.space: "TEC variations filtered within 2–10 min, 10–20 min, and 20–60 min, the Rate of the TEC Index, the Along Arc TEC Rate index, and the vertical TEC"; the "adjusted TEC" product forces all TEC series onto one reference level using GIM — [EGU2020 abstract](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu2020-21635). Usage at scale: 3532 stations, 169,731 TEC series for one event study — Yasyukevich et al. 2020, "Wave Signatures in Total Electron Content Variations: Filtering Problems", *Remote Sensing* 12(8):1340 — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12081340).
- Downstream open code building on tec-suite outputs: PadArt/mosgim2, "Global Ionospheric Mapping with GNSS" — test data are tec-suite TXT outputs — [GitHub](https://github.com/PadArt/mosgim2).
- TuRBOTEC availability: the 2020 paper describes the algorithm in full (equations, workflow, Python scipy.optimize.lsq_linear) but no public repository link is given in the paper, and no TuRBOTEC source repository was located in this session (gap).

### Inferences
- For the reconstruction project, tec-suite (GPL) is a legitimate open reference for the slant-TEC pre-processing stage (leveling, outlier/cycle-slip editing per Blewitt-style algorithms), and SIMuRG API/data can serve as an independent regional comparison dataset.
- The mapper's core (7-coefficient fit + BVLS) is fully specified by the 2020 paper, so an independent reimplementation (as this project does) is scientifically checkable against Eqs. 3–9 and the reported accuracies.

### Gaps
- TuRBOTEC source code not found publicly (as of 2026-11-17).
- SIMuRG's exact VTEC mapping/interpolation algorithm could not be extracted (Springer full-text fetch blocked); its products are documented at abstract level only.

## 7. Recent advances (2021–2026) relevant to a regional TEC mapper

### Takeaway
The active directions are: (a) Kalman-filter-based sequential regional mapping for real time, often with model priors; (b) machine-learning TEC models/forecasters reaching 1.7–5.5 TECU RMSE; (c) spherical cap harmonics and two-layer spherical harmonics for regions; (d) hybrid data assimilation (Kalman + deep learning) validated with altimetry; (e) real-time IGS TEC products now validated against Jason-3/DORIS.

### Cited Findings
- Real-time regional EKF mapper: Tang, J. et al. (2025). "Real-Time Regional Ionospheric Total Electron Content Modeling Using the Extended Kalman Filter." *Remote Sensing*, 17(9), 1568. DOI 10.3390/rs17091568 — "a regional real-time EKF-based ionospheric model (REIM) with a spatial resolution of 1° × 1°" — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/9/1568).
- Kalman-filter GNSS+InSAR local ionospheric mapping (real-time capable): Tondaś, D. et al. (2023). *Journal of Geodesy*. DOI 10.1007/s00190-023-01789-z — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00190-023-01789-z). Older foundational KF ionosphere-monitoring algorithm estimating TEC + biases: Anghel, A. et al. (2009), *J. Atmos. Sol.-Terr. Phys.* — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364682608002812).
- Kalman + deep-learning hybrid assimilation validated against GIM and Jason-3: STD 4.7 / 5.57 TECU — *JGR Space Physics*, 2025, DOI 10.1029/2025ja034893 — [AGU](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ja034893).
- Machine learning: Africa DNN (RMSE 5.5 TECU) — [Moses 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1674984720300537); NN global forecast (RMSE 3.5 TECU) — [Cesaroni 2020](https://www.swsc-journal.org/articles/swsc/full_html/2020/01/swsc190061/swsc190061.html); deep-learning completion of GIMs — Yang, D. et al. 2022, *JGR Space Physics*, DOI 10.1029/2022JA030326 — [AGU](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022JA030326); CNN forecasting of China regional TEC maps (RMSE 1.71 TECU, 6-day GIM forecast) — [Tang 2023, RS 15:3405](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/13/3405); NeuralProphet multi-factor regional prediction RMSE 2.33 TECU — [RS 15:195, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15010195); high-precision China TEC model based on ensemble trees — *GPS Solutions*, 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10291-026-02142-5 (one of the newest found) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-026-02142-5); Transformer-LSTM ensemble TEC forecasting (conference-grade source) — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395685037_Ionospheric_TEC_forecasting_based_on_Transformer-LSTM_ensemble_algorithm).

- Real-time TEC products matured: real-time vs postprocessed IGS GIMs benchmarked with Jason-3 altimetry and DORIS — *GPS Solutions*, 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10291-026-02022-y — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-026-02022-y).

### Inferences
- For the reconstruction, the most relevant "modern successor" route is the sequential/Kalman version of a regional polynomial model (Tang et al. 2025 REIM), which delivers what a batch per-epoch fit cannot: temporal continuity, real-time capability, and built-in state constraints.
- The per-station quadratic mapper can be benchmarked against ML-era standards: current good regional models deliver 1.5–3 TECU vs GIM; the reconstruction's 0.01–0.04 TECU reproduction RMSE is an internal-consistency figure, and its physical validation should target the 1–3 TECU band vs CODE GIM.
- Newest paper found in this review: 2026 (GPS Solutions 10.1007/s10291-026-02022-y and 10.1007/s10291-026-02142-5; Universe 12(2):54, 2026 — DOI 10.3390/universe12020054).

### Gaps
- No 2021–2026 peer-reviewed re-implementation or re-validation of the TuRBOTEC model itself was found (its 2020 citation base is modest; most citing works are by the same group or use SIMuRG data).
- ML-model claims above are from abstracts/snippets; individual architectures' training/validation setups were not audited.

## 8. IRI-2020 accuracy against GNSS VTEC (as an external check)

### Takeaway
Published evaluations consistently put IRI-2020 at roughly 4.6–6.8 TECU RMSE against GNSS VTEC in mid/low latitudes (worse at low latitudes, high solar activity, and storms: up to 10–17 TECU), with a systematic low bias relative to GIMs. It is a useful order-of-magnitude/physical-plausibility check, but far too coarse to validate a reconstruction at the 0.1–1 TECU level; CODE/IGS GIM and dSTEC are the tight external references.

### Cited Findings
- Head-to-head over China, 2008–2020: dSTEC-based accuracies WHU-GIM 3.14 TECU vs IRI-2020 4.57 TECU; "the TEC from IRI-2020 is lower than that from WHU-GIM overall, the discrepancy is more obvious in high solar conditions and low-latitude regions"; TEC correction percentage 60–80% (IRI-2020) vs 80–90% (WHU-GIM); SF-PPP shows up-direction bias with IRI-2020 at low latitudes — He, R., Li, M., Zhang, Q., Zhao, Q. (2023). "A Comparison of a GNSS-GIM and the IRI-2020 Model Over China Under Different Ionospheric Conditions." *Space Weather*. DOI 10.1029/2023SW003646 (open access, CC-BY) — [abstract via Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1029/2023SW003646?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,venue); [AGU](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023SW003646).
- COSMIC-2-based study: "The IRI-2020 model yields a mean absolute error (MAE) of 5.5 TECU and a root mean square error (RMSE) of 6.8 TECU" (vs a POD-based global TEC model) — Lei, Y. et al. 2025, *Remote Sensing*, 17(13), 2322 — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/13/2322).
- Storm-time over North Africa: IRI-2020 "RMSE ~10–17 TECU" while the U-Net assimilation achieves ~5 TECU — "U-Net Based Forecasting of Storm-Time Total Electron Content over North Africa…", *Universe*, 12(2):54, 2026 — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/universe12020054).
- Topside-plasmasphere options (COR2-Gallagher vs COR2-Ozhogin, introduced in IRI-2020) validated against GNSS VTEC from 21 ground receivers and compared to NeQuick — Servan-Schreiber, N. et al. 2025, *Advances in Space Research* (article S0273117724006835) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0273117724006835); related in-situ topside validation: Pignalberi, A. et al. 2025 — [GFZ repository](https://gfzpublic.gfz.de/rest/items/item_5028940_4/component/file_5035190/content).
- Historical reference point: in 2014 the mean RMS between IGS GIMs and IRI predictions was "approximately 10 TECU" (pre-IRI-2020, i.e., IRI-2016/earlier) — cited in "Prediction of global ionospheric VTEC maps using an adaptive autoregressive model", *Earth, Planets and Space*, 2017 — [SpringerOpen](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40623-017-0762-8).
- Independent software-comparison context: a PPP/TEC evaluation reports RMS "below 20 TECU … when compared to CODE, IRI" and 5–25 TECU vs COSMIC-1 (pre-IRI-2020-era software) — *Journal of Applied Geodesy*, DOI 10.1515/jag-2024-0107 — [Degruyter](https://doi.org/10.1515/jag-2024-0107).

### Inferences
- Use IRI-2020 in the reconstruction's validation only as: (a) an echo-test source for simulating observations (as TuRBOTEC did with IRI-2012/IRI-plas), and (b) a gross plausibility bound (agreement within ~5–7 TECU RMS mid-latitude, ~1σ); do not treat IRI-2020 agreement as evidence of sub-TECU correctness.
- For tight validation prefer: dSTEC arc consistency, CODE GIM at the station, and Jason-3 altimetry over nearby oceans if applicable.

### Gaps
- No high-latitude (auroral) IRI-2020-vs-GNSS VTEC RMSE number was captured in this session beyond qualitative statements; the He et al. 2023 numbers are China-focused, the Lei et al. 2025 numbers are global.

## Cross-cutting notes: literature vs the recovered choices

Supported by the literature (TuRBOTEC, Sensors 2020 — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)):
- 7-coefficient model (V, G_lat, G_lon, Q_lat, Q_lon, G_t, Q_t) = TuRBOTEC Eq. 3, with mixed derivatives neglected — exact match.
- Weighting 1/[S·(1+dt²)] in slant space = TuRBOTEC Eq. 6 — exact match, including the ±1 h hard window (Δt = 1 h) and the stated rationale (1/S upweights high elevations).
- Per-station-day structure (daily combined satellite biases) = TuRBOTEC's consistent 24-h solve with epoch resolution 5 min–2 h; zero-mean condition per constellation (Schaer convention) required to fix the rank deficiency.
- Elevation cutoff 10° and MSLM mapping function (h = 450 km) — same as TuRBOTEC; α correction is latitude-dependent (0.87–0.97) and optional.

Caveats the literature raises about exactly this model:
- Spatial validity radius ~10–15° in latitude before errors exceed 10 TECU; longitude errors grow more slowly — do not treat per-station coefficients as a regional map beyond ~1000 km ([TuRBOTEC Fig. 10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)).
- Gradient/time-derivative coefficients are much less accurate than the VTEC level itself (latitude-gradient error up to 1 TECU/deg at equatorial stations).
- Without the BVLS non-negativity constraint, the fit yields negative slant TEC on some satellites; the 2020 paper's constrained variant differs by >1 TECU in ~9% of epochs — if the 2017 original was unconstrained, "bug-for-bug" reproduction preserves that defect.
- GLONASS DCB estimates can disagree with CODE by up to 17 TECU (GPS agrees to ~2–5 TECU); treat daily GLONASS bias outputs accordingly.

Unverified / flagged items:
- Geomagnetism and Aeronomy 2015 page range: Crossref says 763–769; TuRBOTEC's reference list says 790–796.
- Li et al. 2019 (Acta Geod. Geophys.) details are snippet-level (publisher-elided abstract).
- Authors not captured for: "The kriging method of TEC instantaneous mapping" (ASR 2002), "B-spline modeling of VTEC over Turkey" (JASTP 2010), Universe 2026 U-Net paper, GPS Solutions 2026 ensemble-trees paper.
- No TuRBOTEC source repository found; no dedicated 1/S vs 1/S² weighting intercomparison found; no condition-number study of the 7-coefficient per-station fit found.

Newest papers found: 2026 — *GPS Solutions* 10.1007/s10291-026-02022-y (real-time vs postprocessed IGS GIMs, Jason-3/DORIS) and 10.1007/s10291-026-02142-5 (ensemble-trees China TEC); *Universe* 12(2):54 (10.3390/universe12020054).
