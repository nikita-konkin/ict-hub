# Reconstruction matches published Irkutsk TEC method

The recovered AbsolTEC algorithm is not an undocumented legacy artifact — it is the published Irkutsk-group single-station absolute-TEC method, the TayAbsTEC/TuRBOTEC family of Yasyukevich, Mylnikova and co-workers at ISTP SB RAS, matching the 2020 *Sensors* paper's Eqs. 3, 4, 6 and 9 almost verbatim. All three originally cited papers are real and verified: the 2015 *Geomagnetism and Aeronomy* paper (primary algorithm description, paywalled) and the 2015 *Results in Physics* short communication (DCB estimation, open access) introduced the method, and the 2020 *Sensors* TuRBOTEC paper is its mature, fully specified form, which now serves as the reconstruction's peer-reviewed reference spec. The science review finds every recovered processing stage standard for its class except two — the phase-tail-shift spike repair (the one choice the literature actively argues against) and, for the legacy path, whatever leveling variant the 2017 binary actually used — while two legacy constants (the asin IPP longitude and the 6371302.0 m radius literal) are quirks the original authors themselves replaced in their 2020 successor. Parity with the 2017 executable still fails the 0.001 TECU tolerance at roughly **0.0126 TECU vertical-TEC RMSE**, with the gap attributable to unreconstructed preprocessing edge behavior and the old solver's single-precision rounding, not to the model itself ([reconstruction doc](N:\ict-hub\docs\absoltec_reconstruction.md)). For the `--model published` path, the literature supports a short, high-leverage upgrade list: the bounded-variable non-negativity constraint the authors added in 2020, latitude-dependent α, elevation-weighted leveling, arc-split slip handling, and a validation stack of IRI echo-simulation, CODE GIM comparison (~1.7 TECU), dSTEC arc consistency and Jason altimetry.

## Seven coefficients and one weight function: the TayAbsTEC fingerprint

The evidence that the recovered algorithm is the published Irkutsk method is equation-level, not circumstantial. TuRBOTEC Eq. 3 is the reconstructed model term for term: I_M = S·[I_V(φ0,l0,t0) + G_φ·Δφ + Gq_φ·(Δφ)² + G_l·Δl + Gq_l·(Δl)² + G_t·Δt + Gq_t·(Δt)²] + I_DCB — the recovered seven coefficients V, G_lat, G_lon, G_q_lat, G_q_lon, G_t, G_q_t plus one combined satellite bias, with mixed derivatives explicitly neglected in both ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The mapping function matches Eq. 4: S = [cos(arcsin((R_E/(R_E+h_max))·sin[α(90°−θ)]))]⁻¹ with **R_E = 6371 km, h_max = 450 km, α = 0.97** at mid-latitude stations (0.94 high-latitude, 0.87 low-latitude) ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The weighting matches Eq. 6: ω = Θ(t_k−t_ji+Δt)·Θ(t_ji+Δt−t_k)·(1/S_ji)·[1+(Δt_ji,k/1 h)²]⁻¹ with **Δt = 1 h** — the recovered strict ±1-hour windows and 1/[S·(1+dt²)] slant weight, whose stated rationale is that "the 1/S factor causes the measurements at high elevations to produce the greatest contribution" ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The bias convention matches Eq. 9: the satellite-vs-receiver split is a post-fit zero-mean condition applied per constellation "following Schaer" — exactly the recovered behavior of fitting combined link biases and splitting for reporting only ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The official TayAbsTEC documentation seals the identification: the `absolTEC.dia` configuration documents a 10° elevation cutoff, the 0.97/0.94/0.87 correcting coefficient, and output columns I_v; G_lon, G_lat; G_q_lon, G_q_lat; G_t, G_q_t; DCBs — the recovered eight-column DAT format term for term ([gnss-lab.org](http://www.gnss-lab.org/tay-abs-tec)). The method name itself is confirmed by the group's 2017 EGU abstract: "The algorithm is based on TEC expansion into Taylor series in space and time (TayAbsTEC)" ([EGU2017-650](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2017/EGU2017-650-1.pdf), [ADS](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017EGUGA..19..650M)). The 2017-dated binary is therefore almost certainly a pre-publication TayAbsTEC implementation; its name "AbsolTEC" is the truncation of "absolute TEC." Two caveats bound this identification. First, the 2020 paper is a successor, not evidence that every newer detail existed in the 2017 binary ([reconstruction doc](N:\ict-hub\docs\absoltec_reconstruction.md)). Second, the absence of the BVLS constraint matters quantitatively: the unconstrained variant produces negative slant TEC on some satellites (TuRBOTEC Fig. 15), and imposing non-negativity shifts the solution by **more than 1 TECU in ~9% of epochs** ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The reconstruction's `--model published` path should adopt the constrained form; the `--model legacy-2017` path should deliberately not.

## Stage by stage: five standard choices and one real defect

**Mapping function and IPP geometry.** The recovered α-modified single-layer model is Schaer's MSLM (α inside the sine argument), a legitimate published variant: Schaer's canonical fit to the JPL extended slab model uses **α = 0.9782 with H = 506.7 km**, while the Irkutsk group keeps the IGS-recommended **450 km** shell and tunes α against IRI-2012 to suppress the "sharp non-physical TEC growth at low elevations, less than 30°" ([Gulyaeva 2021](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020RS007209), [Yang et al. 2014](https://www.ias.ac.in/public/Volumes/jess/123/04/0767-0778.pdf), [IGS 1998 report](http://gauss.gge.unb.ca/IGS/final/ionopp98.pdf), [PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The 10° cutoff sits inside mainstream IGS practice (10–20° across centers) ([IGS 1998 report](http://gauss.gge.unb.ca/IGS/final/ionopp98.pdf)). The weak points are documented: fixed-shell mapping error concentrates below ~20–30° elevation, reaching 0.68 m per 1 m vertical delay between shell-height choices at 10° ([Yang et al. 2014](https://www.ias.ac.in/public/Volumes/jess/123/04/0767-0778.pdf)); the asin-based IPP longitude is literally the published Klobuchar/DO-229 standard form but is quadrant-ambiguous and polar-singular, which is why modern practice substitutes a two-argument atan2 form plus Klobuchar's polar clamps ([Klobuchar 1987 scan](http://www.ion.org/museum/upload/47-klobuchars-algorithm-for-original-document.pdf), [NTCM-G description](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/sites/default/files/NTCM-G_Ionospheric_Model_Description_-_v1.0.pdf)); and the binary's **6371302.0 m** radius literal matches no published source — every publication uses 6371 km exactly, and the reconstruction's published path already uses the consistent 6371 km/450 km pair ([reconstruction doc](N:\ict-hub\docs\absoltec_reconstruction.md)). Second- and third-order ionospheric terms are negligible for this application: roughly **0.05 TECU per 100 TECU** at L1, up to 0.16% of first order under extreme conditions — far below pseudorange noise, though worth a documentation note ([JASTP 2015 review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364682615001157), [arXiv:0908.1905](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.1905)).

**Phase-code leveling.** Per-arc carrier-to-code leveling with one additive offset per continuous arc is the canonical practice since Mannucci et al. 1998 ([ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998RaSc...33..565M/abstract)), and the recovered behavior is standard. Its dominant error source is code multipath inherited as a systematic arc bias, with effects of leveling "as high as 5 TEC" ([Ciraolo 2007](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007JGeod..81..111C/abstract), [Dyrud 2008](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2007RS003770)). The citable improvement is what TuRBOTEC stage 4 does: level with an elevation-weighted (1/S) mean rather than a plain arithmetic mean ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). Whether the 2017 binary leveled with a plain or weighted mean is not established from the decompilation — a parity-relevant open question.

**DCB separation.** Only the sum of satellite and receiver DCB is estimable without a datum ([Sanz et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10291-017-0634-5)); fitting one combined bias per satellite–receiver link per station-day and splitting into satellite/receiver terms only for reporting via a zero-mean-per-constellation gauge "following Schaer" is exactly the recovered design and exactly TuRBOTEC Eq. 9 ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). Two literature caveats apply to the outputs: the reported split is a gauge choice with no physical meaning for VTEC, and **GLONASS DCBs estimated this way can differ from CODE by 10–17 TECU** (TuRBOTEC reports a peaked 15 TECU difference and max 17 TECU / RMS 8.8 TECU, while GPS agrees at the 2–5 TECU level) because FDMA inter-frequency biases fold into the per-satellite estimates ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/), [EGU 2017 ADS](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017EGUGA..19..650M), [Wanninger 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00190-011-0502-y)). A further documented failure mode: receiver DCBs drift by several TECU over a day with temperature, which a daily-constant bias absorbs only partially and which leaks into the fitted V and time coefficients ([Coster 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/rds.20011)).

**The seven-coefficient fit and weighting.** The second-order space–time Taylor expansion is peer-reviewed, simulation-justified ("using the second order … considerably improves the absolute vertical TEC recovery accuracy"), and lineage-consistent (Lanyi & Roth 1988; Sardón & Zarraoa 1997; Schaer's regional Taylor suggestion) ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The 1/S weighting acts in slant space so residual minimization is approximately in VTEC space, and the ±1 h window is TuRBOTEC-specific with a stated temporal-localization purpose ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). Two limits the authors themselves quantified should constrain how the reconstruction's outputs are used and validated: the per-station fit has a **spatial validity radius of ~10–15°** (errors exceed 10 TECU beyond it, faster in latitude than longitude), and the **gradient and time-derivative coefficients are substantially less accurate than the VTEC level**, with latitude-gradient errors up to 1 TECU/deg at equatorial stations ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/), Fig. 10 and Section 4.2).

**Cycle-slip handling.** Self-contained editing independent of receiver flags is consistent with field practice — the 2015 paper's first method reference is Blewitt's 1990 TurboEdit, and TuRBOTEC performs its own TEC-domain outlier/slip removal as a distinct stage ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1134/S001679321506016X), [PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/)). The recovered 4σ/5-TECU spike repair, however, is **not** standard slip treatment: literature practice either fixes the slip by an integer number of cycles (Blewitt) or splits the arc and re-levels the tail independently; shifting the phase tail by an arbitrary amount can alias ionospheric change into the arc offset after a genuine slip ([Blewitt 1990](https://doi.org/10.1029/GL017i003p00199), [Ciraolo 2007](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007JGeod..81..111C/abstract)). This is the recovered behavior the literature most clearly argues against, and it is the strongest candidate for a deliberate divergence in the published path.

**Validation practice.** The literature's standard stack, in order of tightness, is: IRI echo-simulation (TuRBOTEC recovered simulated VTEC with 0.09–0.35 TECU RMS and simulated DCBs to 0.3 TECU RMS), CODE GIM comparison (TuRBOTEC vs CODE GIM RMS **1.7 TECU** at a mid-latitude station, against an inter-GIM JPL-vs-CODE spread of 1.5 TECU), dSTEC arc consistency (phase-derived differential slant TEC accurate to ~0.1 TECU; WHU-GIM scores 3.14 TECU, IRI-2020 scores 4.57 TECU by this metric), and Jason altimetry (~4.6–5.6 TECU standard deviations in recent studies) ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/), [Hernández-Pajares 2017](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017JGeod..91.1405H), [He et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023SW003646), [Atmosphere 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos15060697), [JGR 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025ja034893)). Two traps stand out: comparisons against a single reference of a different method class can produce 10–12 TECU "errors" that are method differences (TuRBOTEC vs Madrigal/SEEMALA-TEC), so several independent references are required; and the binary's three-decimal TECU output is display precision, not accuracy — the honest expectation for a leveling-based single-station mapper is ~1.5–3 TECU against CODE-class GIMs ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/), [Alizadeh & Schuh 2015](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015EGUGA..1714440A)). The reconstruction has not yet run this stack: physical accuracy is unvalidated, and the 0.001 TECU parity target is an internal-consistency goal orthogonal to scientific accuracy ([reconstruction doc](N:\ict-hub\docs\absoltec_reconstruction.md)).

## Priority fixes: close parity first, then add the 2020 constraints

**Parity with the 2017 binary.** The model is not the obstacle — the recovered equations are confirmed published — so parity work should target the remaining preprocessing and numerics gaps. The compact sample association (the offset between leveled values, time and geometry, and the omitted initial samples) and the exact spike/slip edge behavior are the leading suspects for the ~0.0126 TECU vertical-TEC RMSE, since "its exact edge behavior is not fully validated" ([reconstruction doc](N:\ict-hub\docs\absoltec_reconstruction.md)). The old solver's double-to-single-to-double rounding (`cvtsd2ss`/`cvtss2sd`) inside its dense inverse is a second-order suspect; the original's three-decimal output puts a hard rounding floor on any strict comparison ([reconstruction doc](N:\ict-hub\docs\absoltec_reconstruction.md)).

The GLONASS caveat deserves its own line in any published-model documentation: per-station GLONASS DCBs can differ from CODE by 10–17 TECU because IFBs fold into the per-satellite estimates, and the group's own validation found the TayAbsTEC GLONASS values physically more plausible than CODE-corrected ones, which produced negative slant TEC ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/), [EGU 2017 ADS](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017EGUGA..19..650M)). ## What remains uncertain

Five gaps bound the confidence of this review. First, the full text of the 2015 *Geomagnetism and Aeronomy* paper could not be opened (Springer paywall and bot-blocking), so any equation-level claim about it specifically is inferred from its reference list and the group's own later descriptions; likewise paywalled or unfetched were the internal leveling details of Mannucci et al. 1998, the numeric magnitudes in Ciraolo et al. 2007 and Coster et al. 2013, Wanninger's 2012 IFB magnitudes, Villiger et al. 2019's ns-level DCB claims, and Schaer's 1999 thesis itself, whose MSLM constants (0.9782, 506.7 km) are therefore cited through agreeing secondary sources ([PMC7583837](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7583837/), [Gulyaeva 2021](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020RS007209)). Second, the page-range discrepancy for the 2015 paper (763–769 per Crossref versus 790–796 in TuRBOTEC's reference list) is unresolved, plausibly Russian-original versus translated pagination. Third, no peer-reviewed intercomparison of 1/S versus 1/S² versus sin(e) elevation weighting for regional TEC mapping exists — the recovered 1/S choice is confirmed as the published choice and as standard practice, but cannot be independently cross-checked against a dedicated study. Fourth, no peer-reviewed paper discusses the asin-to-atan2 IPP longitude quadrant fix; the atan2 recommendation is an inference from spherical geometry and implementation practice, explicitly labeled as such in the notes, and the origin of the binary's 6371302.0 m literal remains unexplained by any source. Fifth, several bibliographic details rest on ADS or cross-reference lists rather than individual Crossref resolution: the DOI strings for Zhang et al. 2018, Roma-Dollase et al. 2018, Hernández-Pajares et al. 2017, Durmaz & Karslioglu 2015, Dettmering et al. 2014 and Li et al. 2017 are high-confidence but deserve a final DOI-resolution pass before formal citation ([ADS 2018JGeod..92..401Z](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018JGeod..92..401Z), [ADS 2018JGeod..92..691R](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018JGeod..92..691R), [ADS 2017JGeod..91.1405H](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017JGeod..91.1405H), [ADS 2014JGeod..88.1131D](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014JGeod..88.1131D)). Minor residuals: the Li et al. 2019 model intercomparison is snippet-level only (elided abstract), the 2025 *Adv. Space Res.* mapping-function assessment was available only as an abstract, TuRBOTEC's slip-detection reference [50] could not be resolved, no public TuRBOTEC source repository was found, and no condition-number study of the seven-coefficient per-station normal matrix exists in the literature.

## Conclusion

The reconstruction project's reference problem is solved: the algorithm it recovered is published, open-access, and fully specified, which means the two paths now have two different, equally legitimate objectives. The legacy path's remaining 0.0126 TECU gap is a reconstruction-archaeology problem — preprocessing edge behavior and single-precision solver rounding — not a science problem, and chasing it is justified only insofar as the hub needs bit-parity with the 2017 executable. The published path, by contrast, should deliberately diverge exactly where the original authors themselves diverged between 2017 and 2020: the reconstruction's two-model split mirrors the real evolution of the method, and every upgrade on the science list above — BVLS non-negativity, latitude-dependent α, weighted leveling, arc-split slip handling — is a change the Irkutsk group already made to their own algorithm, validated it, and published. The most useful reframing this review offers is about the target: the reconstruction's 0.001 TECU parity goal and its scientific accuracy goal sit on different scales, since the literature places leveling-based absolute TEC at ~1.5–3 TECU against CODE GIMs and the inter-GIM spread alone is ~1.5 TECU. A reconstructed algorithm that eventually agrees with the binary to 0.001 TECU and with CODE to 1.7 TECU would be, by the standards of this literature, exactly as accurate as the method its authors published — and the way to demonstrate that is to run the validation stack they themselves used.
